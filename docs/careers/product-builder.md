# Product Builder: from product decision to a working change

[English](product-builder.md) · [Русский](../ru/careers/product-builder.md) · [Careers ahead](README.md) · [Atlas map](../README.md)

Product Builder is a useful title to investigate when a role combines product judgment with building. The practical question is what the person must deliver personally: a design, a prototype, a production change or the whole path to continued use. Two public descriptions show why that distinction matters.

## How to check the role

Ask for one recent piece of work completed by someone in the role. Who selected the problem, designed the experience, wrote the code, reviewed it, measured the outcome and supported it afterward? Then compare those responsibilities with evidence you can actually show.

AI-assisted development still requires judgment about data, correctness and failure. A prototype that looks persuasive may not be ready for production. A product manager considering this route should be prepared to explain and inspect the implementation; an engineer should be prepared to defend the user problem and product decisions.

Compared with [FDE](fde.md#what-the-role-owns), the distinction is not prestige or a fixed coding percentage. Inspect the users, the deployment environment and ownership. A Product Builder may own an internal product; an FDE may work inside an external customer's constraints. Both titles can cover substantial engineering.

## One original exercise

Build a small internal tool that helps a fictional team inspect customer evidence. Use synthetic interview notes. Let a user find a theme, inspect its supporting passages and reject an unsupported conclusion.

Deliver a one-page brief, a working prototype, a small evaluation set and a note explaining one decision you changed after testing. Include a misleading passage and show how the tool handles it. A reviewer should be able to trace each conclusion to evidence and reproduce the run. Define what useful outcome you would measure in a real pilot; do not invent realised business impact.

This is an **editorial practice task**, not a Figma or ShipBob take-home.
